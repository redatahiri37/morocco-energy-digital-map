# The cost of storage just crossed the line in Morocco.

**Published version — Article 2/3, "Overbuild vs. Orchestrate" series.
Published: Friday May 22, 2026 (W20). Source: user-provided final
text, filed verbatim.**

---

Two weeks ago I argued that the [Nouaceur's DC announcement](https://redatahiri.substack.com/p/the-grid-plan-hasnt-caught-up) can only expect a smaller grid connection than its press release suggests, if you put a battery in the building.

The open question is cost.

A price line crossed in 2024.

### The Curve

[BNEF's 2025 Battery Storage System Cost Survey](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-energy/battery-storage-costs-hit-record-lows-as-costs-of-other-clean-power-technologies-increased-bloombergnef/) put the global average installed cost of a turnkey 4-hour BESS at $110/kWh. Pack prices for stationary applications fell to $70/kWh, a 45% drop in 12 months. The global benchmark Levelized Cost of Storage (LCOS, after the famous now LCOE) for a 4-hour standalone project landed at $78/MWh*, the lowest reading since BNEF began tracking it in 2009.

In 2020 the same number was over $600/kWh installed.

Following the fears that rose from the Iran's war (3rd Gulf War) we can only expect that the demand for batteries will rise in 2026.

That's roughly 80% in five years, faster than the PV cost curve in its sharpest decade.

### The Morocco Anchor

Back in August 2025, ACWA Power, in consortium with Nareva, won the [Noor Midelt II and III](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/08/06/acwa-power-secures-two-solar-plus-storage-projects-in-morocco/) at a tariff of 0.3245 MAD/kWh for Noor Midelt II and 0.3652 MAD/kWh for Noor Midelt III. That's roughly $32.5/MWh and $36.5/MWh.

Each plant parameters are the following:

- 400 MW PV nameplate,
- 230 MW / ~300 MWh co-located BESS,
- 30-year BOO PPA.

ACWA's bid included 200 battery cycles per year the differentiator that beat Engie's 0.3233 MAD/kWh standalone PV offer.

MASEN paid for storage in the auction outcome.

Sit with that for a moment.

A 400 MW solar plant with a 300 MWh battery, in Morocco, cleared at one-third the unsubsidized U.S. PV-plus-storage floor. No production tax credit. No investment tax credit. No ancillary services market.

Just insolation, land cost, a 30-year offtake, and a developer willing to commit to a duty cycle.

When a developer offers free cycles, they're telling you the BESS pays for itself on the daily charge, discharge arbitrage alone, before any capacity or ancillary stream they may or may not get.

### The Avoided-Cost Side

Now let's do the comparison for Nouaceur.

Let's take back the [first article's](https://substack.com/@redatahiri/p-197702286) sizing:

- 500 MW IT load
- 200 MWh / 50 MW co-located BESS shaving the connection from ~500 MW down to ~350 MW (I did bet 250 down till MW).

At $110/kWh installed, the BESS hardware is roughly $USD 22M.

Being the son of a former importer, let's apply a Moroccan project premium, import duty on Li cells, EPC margin, financing cost at 7–9% WACC (versus 4–5% in the EU). and the all-in number is probably $USD28–32M for 200 MWh on the ground in Nouaceur.

So that's on one side: we store the energy we need to avoid:

1. cost of connexion and transmission
2. fast forward the grid connection
3. secure 200 MWh safe from grid constraints, specially important for an activity that sells continuous availability

The avoided alternative. Let's do a fast back of the enveloppe estimation from a previous project.

100–150 MW of transmission reinforcement at the Casablanca southern ring. ONEE doesn't publish cost-per-MW figures publicly, but the [ONEE and EIB-KfW 2024 financing package did](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/office-national-de-l%E2%80%99%C3%A9lectricit%C3%A9-et-de-l%E2%80%99eau-potable-onee_maroc-onee-tarikhamane-activity-7331584618032218113-GVhQ/?originalSubdomain=fr) :

→ [€300M for 730 km](https://www.utilities-me.com/news/onee-morocco-grid-loans) of new transmission, and benchmark MENA HV reinforcement costs from World Bank project appraisals put the order of magnitude at $1–2M per MW of incremental peak capacity served.

That's $100–300M of Transmission and Distribution capex to serve a peak that a $30M battery could shave.

The engineering works. The cost works, empirically, ratified by both a private bidder (ACWA at Noor Midelt II) and a state TSO (ONEE's 1.6 GWh program). The avoided-cost line is sitting on the table.

So why isn't the announcement specifying a BESS layer at Nouaceur?

A Moroccan operator stacks arbitrage. No ancillary services is paid.

The fix is institutional, not technical. It needs a regulator who has decided that flexibility is a priority file.
