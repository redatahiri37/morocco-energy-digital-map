# The parabole is about to leave the Moroccan rooftop.

**Standalone post — outside the "Overbuild vs. Orchestrate" series.
More bait, less analysis. Target publish: Friday June 5, 2026 (W22).
Hooks the Layer 4 thread from the series close.**

---

Open Google Earth. Zoom in on a working-class Beirut neighborhood in 2018. Beige rooftops, satellite dishes pointing south-southeast toward Arabsat. The pattern hasn't changed since the 1990s.

Now switch to the 2024 imagery. Same street. Same buildings. But every other rooftop is blue.

That's not a software glitch. That's roughly **1.2 gigawatts** of solar PV that arrived in four years. Lebanon went from 90 MW installed at the end of 2020 to [around 1,200–1,300 MW by 2024](https://tcf.org/content/commentary/the-future-of-lebanons-unlikely-solar-revolution/) — a roughly fourteen-fold increase, almost entirely on rooftops, almost entirely without state subsidy or any organized incentive program.

Now do the same exercise on Karachi. Or Lahore. Or Faisalabad. Pakistan imported [22 GW of solar panels in 2024 alone](https://www.cnn.com/2025/05/01/climate/pakistan-solar-boom) — more than any other country in the world that year. Net-metered rooftop capacity went from under 1 GW in 2023 to [4.9 GW by March 2025](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2622248/amp). Lithium-ion battery imports grew alongside.

In both cases the boom didn't come from a clever policy. It came from a household decision repeated millions of times: the cost of buying a kWh from the grid had become higher, less reliable, or both, than the cost of making your own kWh on the roof.

Morocco is next. The timing is shorter than people think.

### What June 9 changes

On June 9, 2026 — three weeks from now — Morocco's solar self-consumption decree under [Loi 82-21](https://medias24.com/2026/03/21/electricity-morocco-opens-self-production-after-three-years-of-waiting-1645893/) takes effect.

For the first time, a Moroccan household or small business can legally:
- install a PV system on its roof,
- self-consume what it produces,
- inject the surplus into the grid, up to 20% of annual production,
- get paid for that surplus at **$0.21/kWh during peak hours and $0.18/kWh off-peak** ([ANRE's published rates](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/02/23/morocco-sets-net%E2%80%91metering-tariffs-for-high-and-medium%E2%80%91voltage-systems/), Feb 2026).

The decree caps total connection capacity at 3,886 MW nationally (72% solar, 28% wind). That's the rationing mechanism. The first wave of installations will get the capacity. Later applicants will queue.

The decree as written covers medium and high voltage today. The low voltage tariff — the one that determines what residential rooftops earn — is the next ANRE decision on the schedule. Realistic landing: late 2026 to align with the announced [2027 structural tariff overhaul](https://en.greentimes.ma/electricity-a-structural-tariff-overhaul-scheduled-for-2027/).

### Why Morocco is different from Lebanon and Pakistan

In Lebanon, the trigger was the [2019–2020 collapse](https://www.arab-reform.net/project/lebanons-solar-energy-boom/) — state electricity unreliable, diesel generator owners charging $0.50/kWh equivalent. People didn't choose solar over the grid. They chose solar over no power.

In Pakistan, the trigger was the [circular debt crisis](https://www.wri.org/insights/pakistan-solar-energy-boom) — tariff hikes that doubled household bills in 24 months, combined with rolling blackouts. People didn't choose solar to save money on a stable bill. They chose solar to escape a destabilizing bill.

Morocco's residential electricity is [$0.127/kWh on average](https://www.globalpetrolprices.com/Morocco/electricity_prices/) (September 2025). Reliable supply. Stable utility. No crisis trigger.

That's the obvious counterargument: nothing forces a Moroccan household to install PV the way the Lebanese household was forced.

True. But Morocco has three structural factors the other two countries didn't:

1. **Sun.** Casablanca averages 5.3–5.5 peak sun hours/day; Marrakech 5.8–6.0. Beirut sits around 5.0. Karachi around 5.3. Morocco produces 10–15% more kWh per panel than Lebanon and roughly the same as Pakistan — at a much higher PV system reliability (less salt, less dust storm exposure than Karachi).

2. **A working installer ecosystem.** Solar manufacturing and installation grew under the Noor and Noor Atlas programs; system integrators are local; supply chain is mature. Lebanon's boom was bottlenecked by smuggled equipment and informal installers. Morocco starts with a real industry.

3. **The decree is pre-emptive.** Morocco regulated *before* the boom — unlike Pakistan, which is now rolling back net metering ([NEPRA Prosumer Regulations 2026](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2632420/amp), Feb 2026) because the unmanaged boom destabilized the utility's revenue model. Morocco's 20% surplus cap and capacity ceiling are exactly the brakes Pakistan had to install retroactively.

### The math for a Moroccan household

A typical urban household consumes 250–400 kWh/month. Annualized: 3,000–4,800 kWh. Bill: roughly **MAD 3,500–5,500/year** (~$350–550/year) at progressive LV tariff bands.

A 3 kWp rooftop system, properly oriented and sized for that consumption profile:
- Installed cost in Morocco today: **MAD 35,000–45,000** ($3,500–$4,500), based on a market that already exists for off-grid rural systems and small commercial. As LV self-consumption opens up, expect 20–25% installer-pricing compression in the first 18 months.
- Annual production at Casablanca insolation: ~4,500–5,200 kWh/year.
- Self-consumption share for an average household: 60–75% of production (no battery, daytime overlap with cooking/refrigeration).
- Annual bill savings: **MAD 2,500–4,000/year** ($250–$400) on self-consumed energy, plus ~$50–80/year on surplus injection at ANRE rates.
- **Simple payback: 7–10 years.** System life: 20–25 years.

That's without a battery and without any subsidy. Add a 5 kWh BESS at $400–500/kWh and payback extends to 10–12 years but the household decouples from evening tariffs and outages — which is the part Pakistanis and Lebanese actually paid for.

For a small commercial customer (workshop, agro-industrial, retail) with an MV connection, the math gets dramatically better because the buy-back rates are already published — $0.21/kWh peak is meaningfully above their retail tariff in some bands. Payback there is 4–6 years.

### What I'd watch

Lebanon's transformation didn't happen at the policy level. It happened on rooftops, building by building, contractor by contractor. The trajectory is visible in satellite imagery — open-source PV detection algorithms on Sentinel-2 and Planet imagery now reliably identify panels at the building level.

When Morocco's L4 unlocks — probably with the LV tariff in late 2026 — the inflection point will be visible in three places:

- **Imports.** Watch Morocco's PV module import tonnage at the Casablanca and Tanger Med customs. Pakistan's boom was visible in customs data 6–9 months before any policy maker acknowledged it.
- **Distribution permits.** Lydec, RADEEMA, and Amendis applications for self-consumption interconnections. ANRE will eventually publish aggregate statistics; in the interim, FOIA-equivalent requests work.
- **Satellite imagery.** Sentinel-2 and Planet-quality bi-monthly captures over Casablanca, Rabat, Salé, Marrakech. The kind of analysis [Ali Ghandour did for Lebanon](https://www.alighandour.org/project/solar-potential-map-for-lebanon/) is replicable for Morocco at low cost.

The parabole is a 1990s adoption artifact. It told you which households had decided to opt into a global media market that the state couldn't yet provide. The rooftop PV panel is the 2030s analogue. It tells you which households have decided to opt into an electricity market the state is still building.

The state is building it faster than the commentariat realizes. June 9 is the start.

### One open question

Morocco's electricity is too cheap and too reliable to recreate the Lebanon or Pakistan trigger. So the boom — when it comes — will look different. Slower at first. More middle-class than emergency-driven. More commercial than residential.

The 2027 ANRE tariff overhaul is the variable to watch. If residential bands rise sharply for high-consumption households (the regulator's stated intent is to redistribute toward heavier users), the payback math at the top of the consumption distribution tightens by 1–2 years. That's where the boom starts.

If the payback hits 5 years for an upper-middle class household in Casablanca or Rabat, the rooftops change.

The map will show it before the press release does.
