# The cost of storage just crossed the line in Morocco.

**Article 2/3, "Overbuild vs. Orchestrate" series.
Target publish: Friday May 22, 2026 (W20). Originally W19 in the
series plan; slipped one week. Article 3 ("flexibility stack") moves
to W21.**

---

Two weeks ago I argued that the [Nouaceur announcement](https://redatahiri.substack.com/p/the-grid-plan-hasnt-caught-up) wants a smaller grid connection than its press release suggests — if you put a battery in the building.

The open question was cost.

When does co-located storage beat the transmission reinforcement it avoids?

It already did. A price line crossed in 2024. The Noor Midelt II award eight months later didn't announce it. It just ratified it.

### The Curve

BNEF's 2025 Battery Storage System Cost Survey put the global average installed cost of a turnkey 4-hour BESS at **$110/kWh**. Pack prices for stationary applications fell to **$70/kWh**, a 45% drop in twelve months. The global benchmark Levelized Cost of Storage for a 4-hour standalone project landed at **$78/MWh** — the lowest reading since BNEF began tracking it in 2009.

In 2020 the same number was over $600/kWh installed.

That's roughly 80% in five years, faster than the PV cost curve in its sharpest decade.

Lazard's LCOS+ v10.0, published June 2025, gives the unsubsidized U.S. range for a 100 MW / 4-hour standalone BESS at **$115–254/MWh**. Wider than BNEF's benchmark because Lazard prices in U.S. financing, soft costs, and oversupply effects — but the floor is well under $200/MWh, and the midpoint is half of what it was three years ago.

> *Global average turnkey BESS installed cost, 2010–2025. The 2024–2025 step-down is driven by lithium oversupply, larger cell formats, and the saturation of Chinese gigafactory capacity. Source: [BloombergNEF, Battery Storage Cost Survey 2025](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-energy/battery-storage-costs-hit-record-lows-as-costs-of-other-clean-power-technologies-increased-bloombergnef/).*

### The Morocco Anchor

August 2025 — ACWA Power, in consortium with Nareva, won [Noor Midelt II and III](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/08/06/acwa-power-secures-two-solar-plus-storage-projects-in-morocco/) at a tariff of **0.3245 MAD/kWh** for Noor Midelt II and 0.3652 MAD/kWh for Noor Midelt III. That's roughly **$32.5/MWh and $36.5/MWh** at current FX.

Each plant: 400 MW PV nameplate, 230 MW / ~300 MWh co-located BESS, 30-year BOO PPA. ACWA's bid included **200 free battery cycles per year** — the differentiator that beat Engie's 0.3233 MAD/kWh standalone PV offer. MASEN paid for storage in the auction outcome.

Sit with that for a moment.

A 400 MW solar plant with a 300 MWh battery, in Morocco, cleared at one-third the unsubsidized U.S. PV-plus-storage floor. No production tax credit. No investment tax credit. No ancillary services market. Just insolation, land cost, a 30-year offtake, and a developer willing to commit to a duty cycle.

When a developer offers free cycles, they're telling you the BESS pays for itself on the daily charge–discharge arbitrage alone — before any capacity or ancillary stream they may or may not get in year 12.

### The Avoided-Cost Side

Now do the comparison for Nouaceur.

Article 1's sizing: 500 MW IT load, 200 MWh / 50 MW co-located BESS shaving the connection from ~500 MW down to ~350 MW.

At $110/kWh installed, the BESS hardware is roughly **$22M**. Apply a Moroccan project premium — import duty on lithium-ion cells, EPC margin, financing cost at 7–9% WACC versus 4–5% in the EU — and the all-in number is probably **$28–32M** for 200 MWh on the ground in Casablanca.

The avoided alternative: 100–150 MW of transmission reinforcement at the Casablanca southern ring. ONEE doesn't publish cost-per-MW figures publicly, but the EIB / KfW 2024 financing package — [€300M for 730 km of new transmission](https://www.utilities-me.com/news/onee-morocco-grid-loans) — and benchmark MENA HV reinforcement costs from World Bank project appraisals put the order of magnitude at **$1–2M per MW** of incremental peak capacity served.

That's **$100–300M** of T&D capex to serve a peak that a $30M battery could shave.

The arithmetic isn't subtle. It's an order of magnitude.

> *Indicative comparison: 200 MWh of co-located BESS (~$28–32M installed in Morocco at 2025 reference cost) vs. 100–150 MW of equivalent transmission reinforcement (~$100–300M, derived from ONEE EIB/KfW financing and MENA benchmark project costs). The crossover happened around 2022 globally. Morocco crossed it later because of a financing- and customs-driven premium that has now closed.*

### ONEE Already Voted

This isn't theoretical. ONEE itself ratified the cost case in 2025 by [calling for expressions of interest on 1,600 MWh of standalone BESS](https://www.energy-storage.news/onee-1-6gwh-bess-tender-morocco/) across ten strategic sites — Kenitra, Settat, Al Massira, and seven others. Commissioning target: end of 2025 through mid-2026.

ONEE is procuring storage **in lieu of** further T&D reinforcement in those corridors. That is the cost case in action. Not a forecast, not a research note — a capex decision by the country's TSO, against its own balance sheet.

If the math didn't pencil, the tender wouldn't exist.

### The Honest Counter-Argument

The BNEF and Lazard numbers are LCOS for utility-scale stationary BESS in mature markets with stacked revenue streams. Morocco is not yet that market. Three premia push the Moroccan curve up:

- **Customs and import duty** on lithium-ion cells and BMS systems, ~5–10% depending on classification.
- **Financing cost.** Moroccan utility-scale renewable WACC clears at roughly 7–9%; European equivalents are 4–5%. Carry that over a 15-year storage asset and the LCOS gap is material.
- **Lower cycle utilization.** Without an ancillary services market, the BESS earns on energy arbitrage only. One to 1.5 cycles per day rather than 1.5–2. That's a 25–30% revenue haircut, which in a project-finance model translates to a higher levelized cost.

Stack the three. The Moroccan-specific LCOS premium is roughly **30–40%** over the BNEF global benchmark.

Apply that to $78/MWh and Morocco's LCOS sits around **$100–110/MWh**.

Apply it to the avoided-cost side and the transmission reinforcement number doesn't move — those costs are already Moroccan, already loaded with the same financing premium.

The crossover holds. Just less spectacularly than the headline number.

### The Tell

Article 1 ended with this: the [Nexus consortium release](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/nexus-core-systems_morocco-ai-innovation-activity-7448330593987158017-osnf) doesn't mention co-located BESS, doesn't mention a curtailment envelope, doesn't mention a connection capacity smaller than 500 MW.

Now the cost answer makes the silence harder to explain charitably.

The engineering works. The cost works — empirically, ratified by both a private bidder (ACWA at Noor Midelt II) and a state TSO (ONEE's 1.6 GWh program). The avoided-cost line is sitting on the table.

So why isn't the announcement specifying a BESS layer at Nouaceur?

The honest answer is that the **revenue stack** that pays for the second and third value streams of a co-located BESS — capacity remuneration, ancillary services — doesn't exist in Moroccan regulation. A Spanish operator stacks energy arbitrage on day-ahead + secondary reserve on REE's procurement + capacity at Iberian auction. A UK operator stacks arbitrage on N2EX + ancillary on National Grid ESO Dynamic Containment + Capacity Market revenue at T-1.

A Moroccan operator stacks arbitrage. Full stop.

That's enough to win a competitive solar-plus-storage tender at $32.5/MWh when the offtake is 30 years and the duty cycle is locked. It isn't enough — yet — to underwrite an independent merchant BESS at Nouaceur with no PPA glue holding the project together.

The fix is institutional, not technical. ANRE issues a flexibility tariff framework. ONEE publishes a settlement protocol. The capacity market gets a BESS category. None of that needs new physics. It needs a regulator who has decided this is a priority file.

### Where This Goes

If you've read this far you can see the structure of the series. The engineering case was Article 1. The cost case is here. What's missing is the regulatory case — the part where Morocco builds the **revenue stack** to monetize the flexibility the engineering and the cost have already justified.

That stack isn't one product. It's a layered set of markets and contracts that runs from the Nouaceur data center all the way down to the smart meter on a Salé apartment building.
