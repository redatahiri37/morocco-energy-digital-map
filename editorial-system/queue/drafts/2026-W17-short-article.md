# Morocco just became a 500 MW AI country. The grid plan didn't.

**Short article — 2026-04-24. ~520 words.**

---

At GITEX Africa Morocco in April, a consortium led by Nexus Core
Systems — with Naver Cloud operating the platform, Nvidia supplying
GB200 Blackwell GPUs, Lloyds Capital financing, and TAQA Morocco on
power — unveiled a $1.2 billion AI data center in Nouaceur, on the
southern exit of Casablanca. Target capacity: 500 megawatts. Supply,
per the announcement, 100% renewable. Full build by 2027.

Every outlet framed it as a digital infrastructure win. It isn't.
It's a grid-planning event that happens to have a Nvidia logo on it.

**The numbers in context.** 500 MW at full build is roughly 7% of
Morocco's peak demand and 4% of installed capacity. A 500 MW flat,
24/7 load is the largest new industrial customer Morocco has
connected in years — and unlike the phosphate corridor, it is
located, financed, and operated entirely by foreign capital.

Phase 1, originally scheduled for 40 MW in Q1 2026, is now reportedly
16 MW activated. The delivery curve from here to 500 MW by end of
2027 implies roughly 240 MW of annual build once phase 1 is
commissioned. For reference, Morocco's total PV additions across all
sites in 2022–2024 ran at about that pace.

**"100% renewable" is a PPA statement, not an engineering statement.**
TAQA Morocco's generation base is weighted toward Jorf Lasfar coal.
A 500 MW flat load served by certified renewables has three possible
structures: a virtual PPA (accounting, not engineering), a dedicated
new-build paired with storage (expensive, multi-year), or grid
averaging with Iberian imports (mechanically workable, but softens
the "100% renewable" claim). The announcement does not specify
which. That silence is the story.

**Three questions the project forces on Morocco's planners.** One:
where in ONEE's published transport plan does a 500 MW flat load at
the Nouaceur node appear? It doesn't yet. Two: what is the PPA
structure with TAQA, and who bears intermittency risk? Three: how
does this coexist with OCP's existing ~1 GW of flat industrial load,
which is also on a decarbonization mandate? Morocco used to have one
flat-load archipelago. It now has two, competing for the same clean
electron pool.

**The honest counter-argument.** Large AI data centers routinely
miss their first public timeline by 18–24 months. Nvidia GB200 supply
is globally constrained; the November 2025 delegation to Morocco
signaled intent, not guaranteed allocation. If 500 MW lands in 2029
rather than 2027, Morocco's planners have more runway than today's
headlines suggest.

What they don't have is a public coordination model between ONEE,
MASEN and TAQA for a grid with two flat-load anchors running on
renewable supply. That coordination model is either being built
quietly or being skipped. Which of those is true matters.
