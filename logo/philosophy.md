# Threshold Geometry

**Philosophy:** the discipline of two ordered systems meeting along a precise edge.

This is the language of marks that live at intersections — places where one regime of order ends and another begins. Compositions favor a single, decisive central glyph held within generous structural margins, rendered with the meticulous restraint of a metalsmith setting a stone. Every line earns its presence; every angle is the product of patient calibration. The visual currency is the crossed bar, the offset axis, the deliberate node — primitive geometries chosen because they read as ancient and engineered at once. The work must look as if it were arrived at after a hundred rejected drafts.

Color operates as polemic, not decoration. Two saturated chromatic forces — one terrestrial, pulled from sun-baked earth and oxidized iron; one electric, drawn from cold star-light and deep-current pigments — are deployed in confident, uncompromised fields. They do not blend; they meet. A third value sits between them as restraint itself: a bone, a sand, a cream — the negative space that lets the binary breathe and the eye register the threshold.

The mark is engineered for resilience. A composition under this philosophy must read at the scale of a thumbprint and at the scale of a façade with identical clarity. Detail is therefore distilled, never multiplied. A single subtle asymmetry — a dash where there could have been a line, a gap where there could have been continuity — carries the full conceptual weight. This is craft as concealment: hours of decision rendered as something that appears inevitable. The product of master-level execution.

Composition anchors to a perfect square. The central glyph holds the geometric center with monumental authority, surrounded by quiet expanses that function as the work's silence. At the margins, a sparse vocabulary of technical marks — a measure line, a coordinate notation, a small index — surfaces at the threshold of legibility. These are not labels; they are evidence. They suggest the form was found, not invented, drawn from a discipline of careful observation rather than a moment of inspiration. The piece behaves like an instrument plate from a science that does not yet exist.

Typography, where it surfaces, is hairline-thin and monospaced, set at the size of a margin gloss. Its role is to ground the form in measurement, never to explain it. The viewer who pauses long enough to read finds a quiet confirmation; the viewer who does not still receives the work entire. This restraint is itself the result of painstaking attention — the kind of editing that takes longer than the drawing.

Above all: the work must feel like a found object from a culture more disciplined than our own. Painstaking attention disguised as inevitability. Quiet authority over noise. The hand of someone at the absolute top of their field, working slowly, refusing the second graphic when the first one is enough.
